RViz

A 3D view of your robot, like RViz: TF, maps, lasers, point clouds, cameras, markers, paths, poses, the robot model and MoveIt’s planned paths, with the Nav2 tools (goal, initial pose and waypoint routes) and a MoveIt goal you move by dragging. The phone and the PC draw the same scene with the same displays.

Creating it

  • Fixed frame: leave it empty to pick one automatically (map, odom… or the root of the TF tree).

  • Add map, laser, robot model and path displays (on by default): adds Map, RobotModel, LaserScan and a Path called Plan on /plan to the Grid and TF every view starts with, ready for a typical Nav2 robot.

Displays

On the phone, displays appear as chips at the bottom, with a dot for their status. Tap a chip to turn the display on or off and long-press it to edit or remove it. Tap Add to add one by display type or by topic (the search lists every topic on the network that can be displayed).

Each display has its own QoS: Best effort, Reliable or Transient local, and you can also change the depth, history, reliability and durability one by one, as in RViz 2. When a display receives nothing, its dialog says why and, if it can, offers a fix with a button (another topic of the same type, or the QoS the publisher uses).

On the PC, the displays are in a panel on the right, like in RViz: Global Options (fixed frame and background), Global Status and each display with its checkbox and its properties, edited in place. Right click a display for Enable/Disable, Duplicate, Rename and Remove (also at the bottom of the panel, next to Add). Drag the panel’s left edge to make it wider; the button at the top right hides it. In a narrow window or a dashboard widget the chips are shown instead: click to turn a display on or off, right click to edit it.

The fixed frame is picked from the frames in TF or written by hand (on the phone, Write a frame… in its dialog; on the PC, type it in Fixed Frame), even one that does not exist yet: to leave a view ready for a robot that is not running. Until it appears in TF the automatic one is used, and on the PC Global Status says so.

Display

Main options

Grid

Reference frame, plane (XY, XZ, YZ), cell size and count, color, alpha, line style and width, offset.

TF

Show names, axes and arrows; marker scale, name size, frame timeout. The TF topics (/tf, /tf_static or a robot’s own, such as /robot1/tf) and which frames to hide: a checkbox per frame, or patterns such as *wheel*.

Axes

Frame, length, radius, alpha.

RobotModel

From /robot_description (transient local). Visual and collision geometry, alpha and an optional mesh server (see below).

Map

nav_msgs/OccupancyGrid with map, costmap or raw colors, alpha and draw behind. Large maps are downsampled to fit the GPU.

LaserScan, PointCloud2, PointCloud

Squares or circles, size, color by intensity, range, x/y/z, RGB or flat color, value bounds, decay time (up to 30 s). PointCloud2 shows up to 500,000 points.

Odometry

Arrows or axes, how many to keep, position and angle tolerance.

Path

Color, line style and width, poses as axes or arrows.

Pose, PoseWithCovariance, PoseArray

Arrows or axes; covariance ellipse for PoseWithCovariance (e.g. /amcl_pose).

PointStamped, Polygon, Range

Clicked points, footprints and range sensor cones.

Marker, MarkerArray

Arrows, cubes, spheres, cylinders, lines, cube/sphere lists, points, triangle lists and text; delete, delete all and lifetime are honored. Mesh markers (MESH_RESOURCE, STL or Collada) are downloaded from the display’s mesh server, as in RobotModel.

Image

A camera image in a panel over the 3D view: sensor_msgs/Image, CompressedImage (topics ending in /compressed) or H.264/H.265 video (…/foxglove, …/ffmpeg, see Camera). Corner, width and alpha. Several panels in the same corner are stacked; drag a panel to put it anywhere and drag its bottom-right corner (or pinch it, on the phone) to resize it.

Camera

The camera image with the 3D scene drawn over it, seen from the camera itself: grid, markers, clouds, the robot… line up with what the camera sees. It uses the camera_info next to the image (/camera/image_raw → /camera/camera_info, or another one) for the projection and the TF of its frame for the pose. Corner and width; the panel is moved and resized like an Image panel. Image Rendering: image and scene, the image over the scene (with Overlay Alpha), only the image or only the scene.

DepthCloud

A depth image (16UC1 in mm or 32FC1 in m) and its camera_info turned into a point cloud, colored by distance or with the colors of another image (Color Image Topic). Max range, max points, point size and style. …/compressedDepth topics (PNG, from compressed_depth_image_transport) work too.

Effort

The effort of each joint in sensor_msgs/JointState as an arc around the joint’s axis (from the URDF in /robot_description): the closer to the joint’s effort limit, the longer and redder; the arrow shows the direction. Radius and width.

GridCells

Flat cells of nav_msgs/GridCells (obstacles, inflated areas…): color and alpha.

Wrench, TwistStamped, AccelStamped

Two arrows at the message’s frame: force and torque, or linear and angular. The angular part is drawn along its axis (right-hand rule). Colors, scale of each arrow and width.

Temperature, Illuminance, FluidPressure, RelativeHumidity

A sphere at the sensor’s frame, from blue (minimum) to red (maximum), with the value next to it.

Trajectory

MoveIt’s planned path (moveit_msgs/DisplayTrajectory on /display_planned_path) played by a translucent ghost robot, built from the URDF and placed where the real robot is. Color, alpha, loop and speed; it can also show the goal of the MoveIt panel.

PlanningScene

MoveIt’s collision objects (/monitored_planning_scene): boxes, spheres, cylinders, cones and meshes, also those attached to the robot. Color and alpha.

NavMap

EasyNavigation’s NavMap (/navmap): a navigable surface of triangles, on one floor or several (ramps, stairs, multi-storey car parks), colored by one of its layers (cost, elevation, occupancy…) with viridis, heat, rainbow or a flat color, blended across cells or cell by cell; unknown cells in translucent gray. The links between floors (stairs, elevators, doors) as arcs colored by type, and the layer updates of /navmap_layers (whole or only the cells that change). Its RViz plugin’s settings are imported from .rviz files.

InteractiveMarkers

The controls of an interactive marker server (<namespace>/update): their markers or, if a control has none, RViz’s arrows and rings. Move them with the Interact tool.

Each message is placed with the TF at its stamp, from the last 10 s of history. If the TF clock goes back in time (a bag playing in a loop, or a simulation restarted with /clock), the history is cleared, as in RViz, together with the points accumulated by the decay time, the Odometry trail and the PointStamped points.

Views and gestures

  • Views: Orbit, XYOrbit, TopDownOrtho, FPS and ThirdPersonFollower (follows a frame, base_link by default). Reset view puts the camera back.

  • Saved views (in the menu; on the PC, at the bottom of the displays panel): save the current camera with a name and go back to it with a tap.

  • One finger rotates, two fingers pan, pinch zooms and a two-finger twist rotates.

  • The background can be dark, RViz gray, black, light gray, white or midnight blue.

On the PC, drag with the left button to rotate, with the right or middle button (or Shift + left) to pan, and use the wheel to zoom.

Tools

  • Goal publishes a 2D goal pose (geometry_msgs/PoseStamped) to /goal_pose.

  • Pose publishes a 2D pose estimate (PoseWithCovarianceStamped) to /initialpose.

Tap the ground and drag to set the heading. The pose is sent in the fixed frame (or map).

On the phone, the ruler button holds the other tools; on the PC, they are all in the toolbar (there Publish Point is Point and MoveIt goal is MoveIt):

  • Publish Point sends the point of the ground you tap (geometry_msgs/PointStamped) to /clicked_point, like RViz. You can drag before lifting your finger to adjust it.

  • Measure: drag on the ground from one point to another to see the distance and how much it changes in x and y. The measurement stays until the next one or until you tap the ruler again.

  • Select works with point clouds and laser scans: tap a point to see where it is, in the fixed frame and in the sensor’s frame, and how far it is from the sensor.

  • Interact works with InteractiveMarkers displays: drag the arrows to move along an axis, a ring to rotate or to move in a plane, and tap buttons and menus. The server receives the same feedback as from RViz (<namespace>/feedback). Touching anywhere else moves the view as usual, and the tool stays on until you tap the ruler again.

  • Waypoints builds a Nav2 route: tap the ground and drag to set each waypoint’s heading. The tool stays on to keep adding them.

  • MoveIt goal shows arrows and rings on the gripper goal of an open MoveIt panel (a tab or a widget): drag the arrows to move it and the rings to turn it, then Plan in the panel. If MoveIt finds no joints for that position, it says so.

You can change the topics of Goal, Pose and Publish Point from the menu, under Tool topics…. See Send Nav2 goals.

Nav2 bar

With waypoints on the map, a bar at the top shows how many there are and how to send them: Stop at each (Nav2’s FollowWaypoints, optionally in a Loop) or Pass through without stopping (NavigateThroughPoses). The buttons remove the last waypoint or all of them, and Go sends the route (Cancel stops it). While it runs, the bar shows the current waypoint, the distance and time left and the recoveries. The route is saved with the tab.

The bar also shows the progress of any navigate_to_pose goal, such as one sent with the Goal tool.

Going back

The Go back button (the clock with an arrow) replays the last minute of this view (its displays’ topics and TF), without having recorded a bag: only on this device, in a loop, with the usual time bar to pause, change the speed or jump. Closing the bar goes back to the live robot. On the PC the view keeps that minute from the start; on the phone the first tap (or Keep the last minute in the menu) turns it on, because it uses memory.

Import and export .rviz

From the menu, Import .rviz file… brings in the displays (every type in the table, groups included; MoveIt’s MotionPlanning becomes a Trajectory), the fixed frame, the background, the current view and the goal, pose and point topics of a config from desktop RViz. Anything it can’t use is listed as Not supported here. Importing replaces the tab’s current configuration. Export .rviz… saves the view as a file you can open with rviz2 -d. See Import and export and Visualize your robot in 3D.

Image annotations

Image and Camera have an Annotations Topic: boxes, circles, lines and texts drawn on the image, from visualization_msgs/ImageMarker (they add up by namespace and id, with their lifetime, like image_view) or from Foxglove’s foxglove_msgs/ImageAnnotations (each message replaces the previous one). The type is found on the network by itself. Coordinates are the image’s pixels. On an Image panel the eye button (or a double tap) shows or hides them; on Camera, the Show Annotations option does.

Robot meshes

URDF boxes, cylinders and spheres are drawn directly. Meshes (STL or Collada .dae) are downloaded over HTTP from the mesh server set in the RobotModel display, for example http://ROBOT_IP:8000: package://pkg/path becomes <server>/pkg/path. Any web server that serves your packages’ share folders works: see Visualize your robot in 3D. Without a mesh server, parts that use meshes are not drawn and the display says how many.
